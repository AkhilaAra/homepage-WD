  1. my website is a shifting house

    this article explains what a website can be and how it can be used. first, it explains that a website is just a file that lives on server somewhere. but it goes on to explain that a website can have endless possibilities. the maker of the website gets to be an author and an architect; writing the code and building the structure of it. a website allows the individual to take back power from big corporations. instead of relying on them to make our space on the internet, we can make it ourselves. create our own world. it can be imperfect and incomplete. the article also mentions multiple metaphors to explain what a website can be. It's like a shelf, a room, a plant, garden, puddle, or a thrown rock. my favorite metaphor is probabaly the puddle since it explains that it can just be temporary collection of our thoughts. It can be burst or a trickle or completely dry. there's a certain comfort in that. it's not something we have to berate ourselves to maintain. our website will progress and grow with us.

  2. a rant on technology

    a rant on technology explains how technology is a lot more important than people give it credit for. technology is how society copes with physical reality. it's our interface with the material world. humans originally just had the natural world and we built on it to make life easier four ourselves. people don't pay attention to more simpler technology such as a lighter than they do to what they consider 'hi-tech'. but that common human invention is technology too. technology is also the root of popular media such as science fiction. it's interwoven with our lives in so many ways. i believe this article relates to our class because it shows how our simple websites are just as important as ones from big corporations are as well. in that way, it also relates to the other article from this week.

  3. a handmade web

    a handmade web is a website that is coded by hand rather using a software like squarespace or wix to create one. these websites are one of a kind and temporary. there also many other connotations that the term evokes. some that carpenter lists are the comparisons between a self-made websites and zines, artist books, engaging in the making of websites, to the physical body, in the labor in making a websites, and finally, is using the smallness and slowness of making a website as a form of resistance. i believe carpenter lists all of these because of human-focused they all are. coding a website by hand is all about self and the individual. making a website has a certain humanity to it that an automatic website maker doesn't.
